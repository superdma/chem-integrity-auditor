## Evidence

**Fig. 2.** One framed panel with five offset traces and no separator rules,
so the generic stack splitter cannot isolate them. Colour-mask extraction of
the purple (0NZO-30-SCN) and red (4NZO) traces, high-pass filtered and
resampled over the 2θ axis, gives a Pearson **r = 0.90–0.93** (window 15–61
px). Visual inspection: same peak positions and nearly the same relative
intensities.

Benign explanation: both samples contain ZnO, so peak positions must coincide.
The correlation is therefore **not** proof of a duplicated segment. What the
notice calls “partially identical” is likely an exact copy of one trace's
detail, which the profile metric cannot distinguish from shared phase. Level 2.

**Fig. 8b.** The before (green) and after (brown) patterns were cropped from
the panel halves and compared:

- `image_dup_orb.py`: **35 matches, 29 RANSAC inliers, ratio 0.83**, same
  orientation.
- `spectral_noise_fingerprint.py`: `curve_r = 0.67`, `noise_corr = 0.35`.

An ORB match across the whole trace is consistent with a partially copied
pattern, but before/after XRD of the same electrode is expected to be similar;
Level 2, raw scans required.

**Fig. 5 (FTIR).** Five offset traces in one framed panel; the splitter cannot
isolate them and the patch scanner finds no copied segment (0 hits). The
allegation is prior publication, which the pipeline cannot test without ref. 1.
Level 3 (untested), not clean.

## Interpretation

Two of the three charges leave a measurable in-figure trace (r ≈ 0.90 and
29 ORB inliers), but both have a strong benign explanation (same crystalline
phase / same electrode before and after). They are Level 2 leads that match
the notice, not independent proof. The FTIR charge is cross-publication and
untestable here.

**Grade: Level 2** for Fig. 2 and Fig. 8b; **Level 3 (untested)** for Fig. 5.
