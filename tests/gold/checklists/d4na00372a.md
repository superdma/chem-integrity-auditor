## Evidence

**Fig. 4 (EDX).** Panel map: 0 = (a) TiN, 1 = (b) ZnO, 2 = (c) TiN-ZnO,
3–4 = the elemental maps in (d).

```text
(a) TiN       ↔ (c) TiN-ZnO : curve_r 0.46, noise_corr 0.81  shared_noise_texture
(a) TiN       ↔ (b) ZnO     : curve_r 0.27, noise_corr 0.21  inconclusive
(b) ZnO       ↔ (c) TiN-ZnO : curve_r 0.22, noise_corr 0.28  inconclusive
```

The (a) ↔ (c) pair is the one the notice calls out. Two spectra of different
compositions share a strong background-noise texture while their peak
profiles differ (`curve_r 0.46`); that is the signature of a shared
baseline/template, not of two independent acquisitions. Together with the
notice's statement that the raw EDX data showed manipulation, this is
Level 3. The b ↔ c claim is **not** reproduced.

**Fig. 4b repeating patterns.** `spectral_segment_dup.py` on the 236 × 161 px
panel returns 0 copied segments. The published panel is too small to resolve
the raw-count periodicity the editor examined; the repeating pattern may exist
only in the raw spectrum. This is a resolution limit, not a negative finding.

**Fig. 3 (FESEM).** Panel 7 = (e) Ni-TiN-ZnO. No ORB pair involving panel 7
reaches the reporting threshold, and no internal duplicate is found. The only
strong Fig. 3 pair is (b) ↔ (b1) (39 matches / 37 inliers, ratio 0.95): the
same ZnO sample at two magnifications, a legitimate pair. Cross-publication
reuse of (e) cannot be tested without ref. 1.

## Interpretation

One of the three notice charges is reproduced at Level 3 (a/c shared noise);
one (b/c) is not supported; one is untestable without the prior article. The
pipeline's contribution is the a/c fingerprint, which is independent of the
editor's raw-data examination.

**Grade: Level 3** for the a/c EDX similarity; **Level 3 (untested)** for the
Fig. 3e prior-publication claim; **not reproduced** for Fig. 4b and b/c.
