## Evidence

Fig. 2 is a 3-trace stack (red / blue / green) inside one framed panel.
`panel_split.py` now recognises this as a stacked figure (1 panel), and
`stack_split.py --keep-color auto` isolates the three traces:

```text
stacks/d5ra01695a-f2/.../band_00_y6-220.png    red    NiCo2O4/rGO
stacks/d5ra01695a-f2/.../band_01_y224-438.png  blue   NiCo2O4
stacks/d5ra01695a-f2/.../band_02_y442-657.png  green  pure rGO
```

- `image_dup_orb.py`, red ↔ blue: **63 matches, 51 RANSAC inliers,
  inlier ratio 0.81, same orientation**, bbox x 67–371, y 91–165.
- `spectral_noise_fingerprint.py`, red ↔ blue: `curve_r = 0.48`,
  **`noise_corr = 0.60`, verdict `shared_noise_texture`**.
- Red ↔ green: `noise_corr = 0.49` (inconclusive); blue ↔ green: `0.38`
  (independent). The green rGO trace does **not** share the texture, so the
  match is not a global plotting/JPEG artefact.
- `spectral_segment_dup.py` on each isolated band: **0 copied segments**
  (patch NCC, window 40 px, threshold 0.985). The within-trace charge is not
  reproducible at the available raster resolution.

## Interpretation

The red and blue traces have different low-frequency profiles (the red trace
has the D/G bands at ~1350/1590 cm⁻¹, the blue one does not), yet they share
high-frequency noise over x ≈ 450–1200 cm⁻¹. That is the signature of a
processed copy of one dataset re-plotted with the peaks removed, not of two
independent measurements of the same phase; two genuine runs would have
independent noise. The green trace is independent, which rules out a
figure-wide rendering artefact.

**Grade: Level 3** — strong, independent pixel evidence of shared data; raw
instrument files are required to decide whether the shared segment is the
authors' admitted mis-insertion (which the notice already accepts) or a
broader reuse. The notice itself supplies the adjudication; the pipeline
reproduces the technical basis.
